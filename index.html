01The problem

You can't defend a plant you can't list.

In August 2025 CISA, the FBI, the NSA and partner agencies in six other countries published joint guidance on OT asset inventories. Its starting point is blunt.

"Without an inventory, organizations do not know what they have." Foundations for OT Cybersecurity: Asset Inventory Guidance for Owners and Operators, CISA et al., Aug 2025 · source

Why it's hard

Scanning can break things

Industrial controllers are fragile. Documented cases show EtherNet/IP modules dropping connections or needing a power cycle after ordinary nmap or Nessus scans. Plants therefore avoid active discovery.

Why it's hard

Inventories are half-filled

The guidance lists 14 attributes per asset. Many, like firmware version, are exactly the ones you need to know whether a published vulnerability applies, and they're often the ones missing.

Why it's hard

Advisories don't reach the list

CISA publishes ICS advisories in a machine-readable format (CSAF) and keeps a Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list. Linking them to a real device still takes manual work.

02The idea

Two ideas, one tool.

Idea 1 makes the inventory honest about what it doesn't know. Idea 2 turns what it does know into a risk list. Each makes the other more useful: the gaps from idea 1 explain exactly why a match in idea 2 is certain or only possible.

Idea 1

Graded inventory

Every asset gets the CISA 14 attributes. Each filled field carries its evidence (which packets, which protocol message) and a confidence level. Each empty field says how to fill it.

+
Idea 2

Advisory matching

Vendor, model and firmware are matched to CISA's CSAF ICS advisories. Anything on the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list is flagged first.

=
ReConIcs

"This is what you have, how sure we are, and what to patch first."

Passive only, runs offline on a laptop from a captured traffic file. Nothing is sent to the plant network, and nothing leaves the machine.

In scope for 10 Oct

  • Passive discovery from PCAP for Modbus/TCP, S7comm and EtherNet/IP
  • Asset record with all 14 CISA attributes, evidence and confidence
  • Completeness score and a "how to fill this gap" list
  • CSAF advisory + KEV matching with three confidence levels
  • JSON, CSV and a single-file HTML report
  • Measured against a labelled lab ground truth

Out of scope (said out loud)

  • Any active scanning or probing. v1 never transmits.
  • Live capture on real plants or the internet
  • Exploitation of anything
  • DNP3, BACnet, PROFINET etc. (listed as future work)
  • A GUI. The HTML report is the interface.
  • Claims about real-plant accuracy; results are lab and public-PCAP only

04Try it

What does one device's record buy you?

This is the core logic of ReConIcs, running in your browser. Tick the attributes known for a device, or load an example, and watch the completeness score and the advisory match level change. The examples are illustrations of typical cases, not measured results.

Completeness against CISA's 14
0 / 14
Advisory match level
EXACT · vendor + model + firmware. The advisory's affected version range can be checked.
PROBABLE · vendor + model. The advisory applies to this product; firmware is needed to confirm.
POSSIBLE · vendor only. Lists advisories for the vendor, as leads.
NONE · no vendor. Nothing to match; fix the inventory first.

05Landscape

Why not just use Malcolm?

Reviewers will ask this first. Passive ICS discovery on its own is not new, and we should say so. What's new is putting the CISA completeness grading and advisory matching in one lightweight, offline tool, and measuring how well it works.

08Rules & risks

The non-negotiables.

Industrial systems control physical processes. The ethics rules below are signed off by the guide in week 1 and apply to everyone.
